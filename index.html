Under the Same MoonDi Bawah Bulan yang Sama

"Father, tonight is colder than yesterday."

"Will the cold air make the moon impossible to see?"

She narrowed her green eyes, trying to steady her sight at the telescope. She stood on tiptoe to bring the telescope level with her small body.

Her father smiled as he watched the adorable ways of his daughter, pure innocence and impatience.

"Cold air won't stop the eyes from seeing the moon, Ganymede."

"The moon can be seen clearly when there is no air pollution."

And just as he said, once she had adjusted her eye at the telescope, she finally caught a clear picture of the moon. Her small hands moved to the knob to magnify the moon, until she could see its surface, full of craters and rocks, rough and dusty.

"Father! I can see the surface clearly!"

"There are so many rocks, and it looks like gray sand too?"

Her father stroked his daughter's black hair with a gentle hand.

"Yes. The moon is covered with rocks, and the sand you see is very fine, dry material mixed with fragments of the rocks."

"If you're interested, I can buy you an astronomy book that explains all of that."

Ganymede turned to her father at once, a wide smile, her green irises sparkling bright, and hugged him tightly. It was the picture of a child's deep love.

"Thank you, Father."

"I promise I'll read it to the very last page."

Her father smiled warmly at that. With one calm motion he lifted Ganymede's tiny body and began carrying her up to the roof of their house. The clock showed exactly eleven at night. When the door to the rooftop opened, their eyes were instantly fixed on part of the moon, just beginning to appear from behind the clouds that had covered it.

"Ganymede, the moon is very beautiful, isn't it? Even though we can see only half of its true shape?"

Ganymede answered her father's words with only an eager nod, without turning her head even slightly. She didn't want to miss the moment the clouds finally uncovered the other half of the moon. Her father, watching, could do nothing to pull his daughter's attention away, so all he could do was laugh softly at the cuteness of a small child's innocent nature.

"When I feel sad, I always look for a place to be alone and watch the sky."

"At the very least, it makes my tiredness and sadness go away."

Hearing that, Ganymede turned at once to look into her father's eyes.

"Are you often sad, Father?"

"Is that why you always come home late every night? And end up missing the time we play together?"

Her father immediately regretted having revealed his "sadness", the moment he heard Ganymede's innocent answer, in her soft voice that sounded disappointed.

"No! Ganymede. I come home late because there's a lot of work I have to take care of. It doesn't mean I want to miss the time when we play."

She narrowed her eyes, her gaze full of doubt.

"Then why can you be sad and look for a place to be alone?"

Her father rubbed the back of his neck, looking a little awkward. But he quickly regained his calm, a nervous smile now carved on his face.

"You know what? In a grown-up's life there are times when the body feels tired and the mind feels heavy. The only way to heal from all of that is to find a place to be alone and do the things you love."

"When you grow up, you'll understand all of it."

Once again, what her father told her was about being grown up. She wondered why her father sometimes told her things that were always tied to the way adults think. It couldn't even be denied that at times the topic sounded boring.

Ganymede's gaze, which had been on her father, turned back to the moon, now no longer covered by clouds. In her eyes the moon looked like a big, shining ball, something very ordinary to other people's eyes. But because her father kept giving her the belief in the beauty and perfection of the heavenly bodies of the universe, Ganymede in the end came to love her father's hobby too, watching the objects in the sky.

Everything in this world is nothing but an illusion.

We are not real, we are only dreaming.

And we will wake up when we die.

"If someday I die and you miss me, then look at the moon."

"That moon is the same moon that we look at together beneath the night sky."

A single tear rolled down, followed by many more. When she turned her gaze to her father's face, the pool of tears made everything look blurred. She leaned her face on her father's shoulder, trying to muffle sobs that in the end could not be held back.

As awareness began to come back, a gust of cold wind brushed her skin. Yet she still did not want to lift her head. Instead, in a quiet whisper, she said:

"No one can predict exactly what will happen in the future, and no one can know how each person's fate will turn out."

"People have all kinds of characters. It's hard to know who is the most sincere. But unexpectedly, the very person whose sincerity I trusted the most, whose love I trusted the most, was the one who could give me the deepest wound."

The frustration she had held in all this time slowly poured out.

"If you really knew that you were the one who left the deepest wound in me, then why do you keep appearing in my memories?"

"Why do you keep letting me drown in sadness? You don't even give me the chance to feel the moments when I should be living my life in peace."

She wrapped her arms around her father's figure, which had begun to vanish. Slowly, it turned into fine strands that drifted away like dandelion seeds. She gave him her last embrace in her normal form, now nineteen years old.

"If your coming only brings wounds, then please, don't come to me even in my dreams, Father."

Everything began to collapse. The wind formed a whirlwind that pulled everything up into the vast, dark sky.

Her father's figure had vanished. Ganymede's body slumped, both hands covering her face, both knees touching the ground where she stood. All that remained was herself, surrounded by darkness and the quiet sobbing of the pain of loss.

When at last the wave of consciousness fully reached her, her closed eyes began to open, adjusting their sight to the tears that were still brimming in real life.

Both hands wiped her eyes at once to clear away the traces of the tears. She sat up on her bed, finding herself awake on a Sunday morning. Her green eyes then turned to the wall clock in her room.

Seven in the morning.

"I hate this damn Sunday."

StepfatherAyah Tiri

Sunday mornings felt boring to Ganymede. Honestly, she hated them.

To get rid of the boredom, she often read books. Everything from astronomy, human anatomy and fantasy stories, to her favorite, dark fairy tales.

After showering and putting on casual clothes, she stood in front of the bookshelf to look for a book that might chase away the boredom this morning. In the end, her fingers reached out and took an old fairy tale book published around the 1980s. Its cover was dark blue, with a little brown and cream mixed in, giving it the distinct look of an old book.

"The Juniper Tree, by the Brothers Grimm."

Ganymede took the book to read. With long strides she reached her study chair and sat down calmly, as her fingers opened the pages and began to read.

Once upon a time, there lived a husband and wife who wanted a child very much. They waited a long time, until at last the wife became pregnant and gave birth to a baby boy.

But their happiness did not last long. Soon after the baby was born, the mother died. Before she died, however, she prayed that her child might one day be under that tree and stay close to her. And so, after she passed away, her body was buried beneath that tree.

Years went by and the father married again, to a woman who then bore him a daughter.

At first the family seemed fine. But the stepmother came to love her own daughter very much, and felt that her stepson would stand in the way of her daughter's inheritance.

Until one day she plotted a murder. She kept a large chest filled with apples and asked the boy to fetch one.

As the boy bent down to take an apple, the stepmother dropped the lid of the chest and killed him. The stepmother tried to make it look like an accident, and even used the event to make her own daughter believe that she was the cause of her brother's death.

The stepmother then cooked the boy's body and served it to the father without telling him what had happened. The little girl was filled with guilt and fear.

The boy's bones were buried beneath the juniper tree at their house. From that tree a bird appeared, the boy's soul in a new form. It flew in every direction and sang a song that told of his death.

His singing won the sympathy of the people who heard it, and the bird was given a golden chain, a pair of red shoes, and a millstone, which became part of his revenge on the stepmother. The golden chain was given to his father, the red shoes to his sister, and then he dropped the millstone on the stepmother, and she died.

After he had taken his revenge, the bird changed back into the boy. At last he was reunited with his family and they lived happily.

Ganymede smiled in satisfaction after reading the last line of the story. Even though she had read this tale over and over, her boredom was cured at once. The juniper tree tale was one of her favorites among all the dark stories she had ever read.

She leaned back against the chair. A rough sigh left her mouth, and that was when her thoughts suddenly drifted back to a certain night when she had quarreled with her mother. The quarrel had come from the different points of view of her and her mother.

Her mother wanted to live a new life with a new family so she would no longer be reminded of the shadow of her late husband, who had died five years ago. So in the end she had begun a relationship with a single man she met at a charity event. They had started out as friends, then realized they suited each other and became seriously involved.

Unfortunately, Ganymede, who was still traumatized by her father's death, felt that her mother had betrayed their family. That made her refuse, fiercely, to give her blessing to her mother's relationship with her boyfriend. From then on, the relationship between mother and daughter grew distant, and it was still that way now.

After a moment, the sound of a knock brought Ganymede out of her daydream. She turned toward the sound, and at that moment the door opened and the first thing she saw was her younger sister standing in the doorway.

Ruby.

"Mother asked me to call you for breakfast."

Without needing permission to come in, Ruby walked casually through the room and lay on the bed, on her side, one hand propping up her head to face Ganymede.

Seeing that, annoyance flashed in Ganymede's eyes.

"I never gave you permission to enter my room."

With that, she quickly put her books back on the shelf. Ruby only gave a hum in answer to her sister's curt manner.

"You're making yourself late on purpose to avoid meeting Mother, aren't you?"

A brief silence settled over the room, until finally Ganymede answered.

"I'm not hungry yet."

Those three words sounded plainly like the classic excuse (a lie). Ruby only laughed softly, not believing it at all.

"You can't lie to me. You're hungry, admit it. You just don't want to have breakfast because the fight with Mother wore down your ego."

Ganymede did not respond and did not deny it either, her eyes staying on the books she was tidying. That was when Ruby spoke again.

"Actually, I also wanted to tell you that tonight we'll have dinner with Mother's boyfriend. If I remember right, his name is Eugene."

Eugene. His full name was Eugene Estern. Ganymede knew his full name from reading her mother's diary.

He was her mother's boyfriend, three years older than their mother. He had gentle eyes, which matched his friendly personality perfectly. Even so, Ganymede did not like him. She hated every man who would become her stepfather, because deep in her heart no man could ever match her late father in the role of head of the family.

Ganymede finished tidying the books that had been stacked in a mess. Her gaze then turned to her sister, who was watching her with an innocent face.

"Ruby."

"Do you like that man?"

It was a simple question, with no hostility in it. She only wanted an honest answer, and Ruby smiled warmly to ease the tension her sister felt.

"Honestly, he's a very generous man. If there were a scoring system for his behavior, I'd give him an 8."

Ganymede listened, her mood still no better, without a smile. She sat down beside Ruby with her arms folded, a gesture of unease filled with all kinds of bad thoughts.

"You like him only because of the expensive things he gives you? That doesn't show that you like him sincerely as a father."

"Liking someone for money and expensive things means you're just using him, right?"

Ruby laughed at that, her cheerful laughter filling the room, before adding:

"Nobody can replace Father's place in both our hearts. And pretending to like someone just for your own satisfaction, isn't that fine too?"

She shifted her body and sat beside her sister, their shoulders touching.

"Mother gives us so little pocket money that to buy even a few new things we have to set aside and save for a long time."

"So there's nothing wrong with taking advantage of this precious moment, right?"

Ganymede fell silent, thinking about the idea of using a stepfather. That was when she remembered again the collection of dark fairy tales she had always read since she was little. From the creepier version of Snow White, to Cinderella's stepsisters cutting off their own feet, to the story of the girl without hands and her life full of suffering.

So many stories told of wicked step-parents, and of characters in fairy tales always ending in tragedy. It had influenced her and left her with trust issues.

One thing she believed firmly was that her instincts rarely missed. Quietly, she also held the belief that her future stepfather was a man full of deceit, who used a mask of kindness to cover his true wicked character.

"Before long, that man's hypocrisy will be exposed." The words came out of her mouth without her realizing it.

Hearing that, Ruby only let out a rough sigh. She did not want to be involved in any of the plans her sister was going to carry out. She then stood and pulled Ganymede's arm so she would stand too.

"Honestly, I don't want to get involved in your crazy plan. For now we should have breakfast, because I'm starting to get hungry."

With that, the two of them finally walked out of the room and down the stairs to the kitchen.

Morning sunlight came into the kitchen, giving a warm and comfortable feeling.

On the plates were already served toast topped with egg, neatly sliced avocado, fresh tomato slices and beef, drizzled with an appetizing honey mustard sauce. Glasses of warm milk were lined up neatly beside them, and in the middle of the dining table was a basket of fresh fruit, free for anyone to take.

Teresa sat in her own chair at the table, sipping coffee with her toast half bitten on her plate. A faint smile appeared on her face when she saw her two daughters enter the kitchen.

"Good morning."

Ruby's cheerful greeting broke the silence, and she received a warm smile and greeting from their mother.

"Good morning, sweetheart. It seems you haven't washed your face again? Even though I've reminded you over and over."

Ruby smiled widely, and the two of them pulled out chairs and sat in their places.

"I'll wash my face later. What matters right now is how I deal with my empty stomach."

She then took a big bite of her toast. And Ganymede? She ate her breakfast in silence. No warm greeting, just ignoring what she thought was not worth doing.

Their mother noticed how quiet she was. She could not deny that she was disappointed by Ganymede's rebellious attitude and her refusal to listen to advice, but she had decided not to drag this problem out, and to mend their relationship so that it would become close again.

"Ganymede..."

The gentle call from her mother made Ganymede turn, though there was no reply yet. Only a hum that carried the silent question, "What is it?"

Without small talk, her mother came straight to the heart of the matter, with no need for long.

"It's about the fight we had a while ago. I realize that I was too emotional then, and maybe there were things I said that offended you."

"Moreover, we are mother and daughter. It isn't right for us to drag this problem out."

Ganymede nodded, then took the last bite of her breakfast, which she had deliberately eaten fast. Her hand picked up the glass of milk and finished it in a few gulps. She then wiped her mouth with a tissue and looked at her mother's face with a serious expression.

"I admit it isn't comfortable to live in the same house without talking. Making peace is fine, especially since you are still my mother."

Her mother smiled at that, relieved, believing all this conflict was about to end.

"But before that, I may need to ask you a few things about Eugene Estern."

Ruby choked at once on hearing that. A conversation like this would not bring any resolution, and would only make the problem wider. It was all clear on their mother's face, which had turned shocked and began to get angry.

"Ganymede! Did you read my diary again?!"

Ganymede showed no fear. On the contrary, she leaned back in her chair with one leg raised, a confident gesture that seemed to say, I'm not afraid of you.

"That's your fault for not locking your room and your cupboard properly, so I could find your important things."

Her mother's emotions overflowed fast. She hated people with no manners.

"I never taught you to be so brazen as to snoop into someone else's diary! You casually read a person's private things as if it were nothing!?"

Ganymede did not want to lose the argument, and she let out her own irritation at her mother.

"You mean you wanted me to learn the fact that you're depressed, and then pour out all your complaints by writing them down?"

"Saying it out loud to get my pity and understanding? Is that it?"

Then came the sound of a hand slamming the table, making Ruby jump with a tense face. But the shout had no effect on Ganymede.

"Venting your feelings in a diary is not wrong at all. You don't know how tiring it is for me to raise a stupid child who is always rebelling!"

"You should be more grateful that you still have a mother who takes care of all your needs!"

Hearing that answer, Ganymede's own temper flared. She leaned forward with her arms folded. Her face showed deep disappointment as she spoke of the sad facts her mother had been doing all along.

"Taking care of my needs? Everything Father left to me as an inheritance, you've already sold off to buy jewelry and expensive things."
